Privacy

Your writing stays on your computer. JustWrite has no accounts, no adverts and no tracking.

Last updated 18 October 2026

JustWrite is made by an individual developer in the United Kingdom, who publishes it as Six Kingdoms Publishing. This page says what JustWrite sends over the internet, why, and who receives it. It applies to every copy of JustWrite 2, whether you downloaded it from this website or got it from the Microsoft Store.

What never leaves your computer

  • Your books: their text, titles, pictures, versions, notes and settings. They're ordinary files in your books folder. If you keep that folder in Dropbox, OneDrive or iCloud, those services' own privacy terms apply.
  • Scanned pages you import. JustWrite reads them on your computer; nothing is uploaded.
  • Your writing history and goals, your personal dictionary, and JustWrite's log of problems.

JustWrite doesn't collect statistics about how you use it, and it doesn't send crash reports. Help › Report a Problem saves a report on your computer; it's only sent if you choose to email it to me, and it never contains your writing.

What JustWrite sends, and to whom

  • Your licence key, to Polar. When you enter a licence key, and about every 14 days after that, JustWrite asks Polar (who sell JustWrite for me) whether the key is valid. It sends the key, your computer's name (for example "DAVID-PC", so you can see your computers in your Polar account), a code that identifies this copy of JustWrite without saying anything about you, and which version of JustWrite and which system (Windows or Mac) it is. During the free trial, nothing is sent to Polar.
  • Buying JustWrite, with Polar. Buying opens Polar's checkout in your web browser. Polar takes your name, email address and payment, and sends you your licence key. I see your name, email address, country and what you bought, so I can help with your licence. Polar's privacy policy applies to the checkout.
  • Spelling dictionaries, from jsDelivr. When you add a spelling language, JustWrite downloads it from jsDelivr, a public download service, which sees your computer's internet (IP) address, as any website does.
  • Updates (the copy from this website). When it starts, and every 12 hours while it's open, JustWrite looks on this website for a newer version, and downloads it from GitHub, which hosts the website and the downloads. GitHub sees your computer's internet (IP) address and which version of JustWrite you have. GitHub's privacy statement applies.

If you got JustWrite from the Microsoft Store

The Microsoft Store downloads, installs and updates JustWrite, so JustWrite doesn't look for updates itself. Microsoft does that under the Microsoft Privacy Statement. Microsoft gives me summary reports, such as how many people installed JustWrite and its ratings and reviews, and reports of crashes that don't say who you are. It never gives me anything you've written. Licence keys, buying and spelling dictionaries work as described above.

This website

This website is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, analytics or adverts, and its fonts are part of the site. GitHub may keep records of visits, such as internet (IP) addresses, under its privacy statement.

How long it's kept, and your rights

Polar keeps your purchase and licence records for as long as your licence exists and the law requires (for tax records, usually six years). I don't keep a separate copy. You can see your licence, its computers and your receipts at any time in your Polar account, and remove a computer there or in JustWrite (Help › Licence).

Under UK and EU data protection law you can ask to see the personal information I have about you, to have it corrected or deleted, or to object to how it's used, and you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk). I use your information only to provide and support your licence; I don't sell it or share it with anyone else.

To ask about your information, or anything else on this page, email me at laowaidave@gmail.com.

Changes

If JustWrite starts to send anything new, this page will say so before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
